# Ethical engagement and retention

ArenaSports should become a habit because it reliably helps communities compete, not because it manipulates players.

## Value loops

### Player loop

Discover relevant tournament -> understand rules -> register -> receive fixture -> coordinate -> play -> submit/confirm -> see table/history update -> find next meaningful competition.

### Organizer loop

Create from template -> recruit -> monitor readiness -> resolve exceptions -> complete competition -> review operational analytics -> improve the next event.

### Community loop

Recurring season -> recognizable rivals -> transparent standings -> shareable stories -> fair recognition -> larger healthy participation.

## Healthy engagement features

- personalized tournament discovery;
- followed organizers and players;
- fixture/reminder calendar;
- clear progress through a competition;
- verified achievements tied to real outcomes;
- form and head-to-head history with context;
- seasonal summaries;
- organizer reliability indicators;
- community challenges without money;
- share cards that do not expose private data;
- optional streaks based on fair play/completion, with forgiving pauses;
- notification preferences and quiet hours;
- "take a break" controls and easy opt-out.

## Prohibited dark patterns

- fabricated scarcity or fake participant counts;
- countdowns that reset deceptively;
- forced daily loss of earned status;
- hidden notification re-enablement;
- guilt language for inactivity;
- randomized paid rewards;
- burying account deletion or notification controls;
- misleading "verified" labels;
- ranking people by invisible suspicion;
- making dispute filing intentionally difficult;
- rewarding harassment or unhealthy volume.

## Recognition design

Recognition should favor:

- completed verified matches;
- sportsmanship;
- reliable attendance;
- helpful organizing;
- fair dispute behavior;
- season performance with sufficient sample size.

It must not permanently shame new players or expose safety sanctions beyond policy. Reputation components should be visible and explainable.

## Notifications

Every notification must answer:

- Why am I receiving this?
- What action is useful?
- When is the actual deadline?
- Can I change this category?

Critical competition state remains available in-app if push delivery fails. Quiet hours respect the user's timezone. Marketing and competition operations are separate consent categories.

## Measurement

Useful metrics:

- verified match completion;
- tournament completion;
- repeat organizer rate;
- time to first meaningful competition;
- dispute/no-show rates;
- healthy notification engagement;
- successful low-bandwidth flows;
- user-reported trust.

Guardrails:

- opt-outs;
- report/harassment rate;
- late-night usage pressure;
- crash and retry frustration;
- appeals and overturned decisions;
- evidence exposure;
- account deletion difficulty.

Do not optimize a metric by hiding failures or removing user control.

## Experiments

Experiments require a hypothesis, eligible cohort, success metric, safety guardrails, duration, stop condition, and analysis plan. Never experiment with weaker security, hidden rule changes, deceptive consent, or reduced appeal rights.
